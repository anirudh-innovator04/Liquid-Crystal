Why LCD Image Quality Degrades at Angles

Liquid Crystals are optically anisotropic (birefringent) rod-like molecules. Light propagation depends heavily on the angle between the light ray and the crystal director axis.

1. Optical Path & Retardation (Δn · d)

When light enters perpendicular (0°), it experiences the calibrated refractive index thickness d. At oblique angles (θ > 0°), the ray travels diagonally through a longer effective path length d' = d / cos(θ), altering the phase shift between ordinary and extraordinary rays.

2. Polarizer Crosstalk & Light Leakage

Subpixel black levels rely on crossed polarizers (90° orientation) completely blocking light. Oblique rays experience an elliptical polarization state shift due to phase retardation error, allowing unwanted light leakage in black states, destroying contrast.

3. Panel Technology Comparison

  • TN (Twisted Nematic): Molecules twist vertically out of plane. Viewing from below aligns your eye directly with the crystal optical axis, causing severe color inversion.
  • VA (Vertical Alignment): Molecules stand vertically when off. Wide angles look past the long axis, leading to gamma shift and contrast loss.
  • IPS (In-Plane Switching): Molecules rotate strictly parallel to the glass substrate. The effective refractive index remains uniform at almost all viewing angles.
Birefringence Retardation Equation
Γ(θ) = (2π / λ) · Δn(θ) · [d / cos(θ')]

Where Γ is optical phase retardation, Δn is birefringence, d is cell thickness, and θ' is the internal refraction angle.
